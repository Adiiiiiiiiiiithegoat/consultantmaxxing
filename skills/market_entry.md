# Playbook: market entry (new market, product, geography or business)

## Clarify first
- Objective of entry (growth, profit, strategic/self-reliance, diversification) and the
  success metric and horizon (e.g. payback within N years).
- Client capabilities: related experience, assets, budget limits, risk appetite.
- What exactly is entering where: product, customer segment, geography, B2B or B2C.
- Has anyone tried and failed? Why?

## Structure
1. Market attractiveness: size and growth, profitability, competitive intensity, entry
   barriers, regulation, demand trends.
2. Client fit and ability to win: capabilities, differentiation, right to win.
3. Feasibility: financial (investment, unit economics, break-even, payback) and
   operational (value chain, sourcing, talent, technology, distribution).
4. Entry mode: build (greenfield), buy (acquisition), partner (JV/alliance/licensing),
   with exit options.
5. Risks and mitigations.
- **When it doesn't fit:** if the client has already decided to enter, skip attractiveness
  and go straight to feasibility and mode; if the "entry" is a new product for existing
  customers, treat it as a growth/launch case.

## Common patterns (frequency in 7 training cases)
1. The answer turns on the entry mode: partner or acquire when the client lacks a key
   capability or local presence (4/7).
2. A quantified feasibility test decides it: payback or break-even against the
   objective's horizon, capturable share of the target market (3/7).
3. Learn from prior failures in the market and design around them.

## Analyses
`top_down` / `bottom_up` market sizing (cross-check both), `break_even_units`,
`npv` / `irr` / `payback_period`, `scenario_table` for share and price assumptions.

## Traps
- Sizing the whole market rather than the capturable share.
- Not stating the objective's horizon, so "profitable" is undefined.
- Assuming capabilities the client doesn't have.
- Not checking assumptions with the client before building numbers on them.

## Definition of done
Go / no-go (or which option) with the deciding numbers (size, share, payback/NPV), the
recommended entry mode and why, key risks with mitigations, and the first steps.
