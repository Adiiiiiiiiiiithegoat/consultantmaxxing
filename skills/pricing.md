# Playbook: pricing (set or change a price)

## Clarify first
- Objective: maximise profit, gain share, recover an investment fast, signal premium?
- The product's difference vs alternatives and substitutes; is it a first of its kind?
- Current price, volumes, cost structure (fixed vs variable, one-off R&D), target margin.
- Who decides the purchase, and what do they currently pay in total (including usage,
  installation or replacement costs)?

## Structure: three anchors, then reconcile
1. Cost-based floor: unit cost + required margin; recover one-off investments over a
   realistic volume and period.
2. Competitor/substitute reference: where alternatives sit; irrelevant if none exist.
3. Value-based ceiling: the customer's total economic benefit (e.g. lifetime cost
   avoided), adjusted by surveys of perceived value.
The price sits between floor and ceiling; the objective decides where.
- **When it doesn't fit:** for a price *change*, the core question is elasticity: what
  volume and growth do we lose or gain, and what happens to profit.

## Common patterns (frequency in 4 training cases)
1. Cost-plus with investment recovery decides it, checked against perceived value (2/4).
2. Value-based premium capped by competition and substitutes (1/4).
3. A price rise looks good per unit but kills growth: profit falls once the volume
   response is modelled (1/4).

## Analyses
Unit cost and margin build-up, `break_even_units`, value-to-customer calculation,
`scenario_table` / `sensitivity` over price and volume response, profit over the horizon.

## Traps
- Recovering an investment over an arbitrary period instead of the asset's real life or
  the client's stated horizon.
- Ignoring capacity utilisation when spreading fixed costs.
- Quoting willingness-to-pay without the customer's full cost of the alternative.

## Definition of done
A recommended price (or range) with the floor, reference and ceiling shown, the volume
assumption behind it, the profit impact over the horizon, and the main risk.
