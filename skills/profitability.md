# Playbook: profitability (profits, margins or market share falling)

## Clarify first
- What exactly moved: profit, margin %, revenue, market share? By how much, since when?
- Is it the client only, or the whole industry? (In the training cases the problem was
  client-specific about 3 times in 4; if peers are fine, look inward first.)
- Business model: where in the value chain, which products/segments, which channels,
  which geographies. Ask for the revenue and product mix.
- Any recent change: new market, new channel, policy, pricing, supplier, ownership?

## Structure
Profit = Revenue - Cost. Split revenue into price x volume (and mix), volume into
customers x frequency x basket, and costs along the value chain (inputs, production,
logistics, selling, overheads), separating fixed from variable.
- Check revenue vs cost at the top first, then go deep only where the numbers move.
- When volume is down, split supply-side (can we deliver?) from demand-side (do they want
  it?). For demand, walk the customer journey: need, awareness, access, affordability,
  purchase experience, after-sales.
- **When it doesn't fit:** "market share" and "fee income" cases often turn out to be
  revenue-driver cases; unusual revenue streams (fees, interconnect charges, payouts)
  need their own equation before any framework.

## Common root causes (frequency in 13 training cases)
1. A breakdown somewhere in the customer journey or channel: stock/range gaps, slow or
   poorly briefed sales agents, unreliable equipment, wrong price displays (4/13).
2. Weak awareness or trust versus rivals: no digital marketing, regional brand vs
   national players, poorly motivated dealers, the wrong selling message (3/13).
3. Costs pushed up by the client's own policy or mix change: an operating rule, a new
   low-value customer segment, a rival's change raising our fees (3/13).
4. External shifts specific to the client: environmental reputation, political sentiment
   about country of origin, substitution in how customers consume the product (2/13).
5. A competitor's cost innovation makes our price uncompetitive (1/13).

## Analyses (sandbox templates)
`margin_bridge` (base vs current P&L), `price_volume_mix`, unit economics per segment,
break-even if a fix needs investment; benchmark each driver against peers.

## Traps
- Diving into costs before establishing which side moved.
- Forgetting product mix or segment mix when revenue changes.
- Ignoring "why only us / why only here?": a client-specific or country-specific
  problem is the biggest clue.
- Stopping at the symptom ("volume fell") instead of the mechanism.
- Ending without recommendations: always give short-term and long-term actions.

## Definition of done
The mechanism is named and supported by data at each level of the tree; the size of the
profit gap it explains is quantified; recommendations address the mechanism, with
short-term and long-term actions, owners and expected impact.
