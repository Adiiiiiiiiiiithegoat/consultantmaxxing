# Skill: strategy concepts

- **Competitive advantage** is relative (to rivals), usually temporary, and shows up as
  above-normal returns. Ask what stops a competitor from copying it.
- **Industry structure** (rivalry, buyer power, supplier power, substitutes, entry
  barriers) sets how profitable an industry can be; position the client where those forces
  are weakest or can be shaped.
- **Economies of scale** matter most with high fixed costs and operating leverage
  (airlines, autos, telecom) and create a minimum efficient scale that deters entry; they
  matter little where fixed costs are low.
- **Economies of scope:** related products share production, distribution and marketing,
  lowering cost and increasing reach.
- **Supplier dependence:** a sole supplier has pricing power and may integrate forward.
  Mitigate with second sources, long-term contracts or partnerships, or developing
  substitutes.
- **Network effects:** value grows with users, which justifies early losses to reach scale,
  but can plateau (enough supply already) or turn negative (quality dilution).
- **Switching costs** make customers stay even when a rival is cheaper: exit penalties in
  contracts, staff retraining, incompatible hardware or formats, painful data moves, and
  brand loyalty. Before a price rise, strengthen them and explain the value; offer
  incentives for longer commitments.
- **Right to win:** for any growth or entry move, name the specific capabilities or assets
  that let this client win there.
- **Implementation:** a strategy isn't finished without sequencing, owners, dependencies,
  milestones, risks with mitigations, and the metric that will show it is working.
